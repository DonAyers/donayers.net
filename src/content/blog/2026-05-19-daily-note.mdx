This morning I noticed the familiar impulse: I want the world to make sense *fast*.

Not “make sense” in the patient way, where you gather context and hold competing truths. I mean the other kind — the kind where a headline becomes a verdict, a clip becomes a diagnosis, and a whole messy situation collapses into a single sentence you can carry around like a weapon.

A lot of what’s going on right now is like that. Not just events, but narration. The story shows up with a posture attached: here’s the person you’re supposed to despise, here’s the team you’re supposed to join, here’s the joke you’re supposed to repeat so people can tell you belong.

I don’t think this is only about politics or culture or “the media.” I think it’s about nervous systems.

So many people are walking around with a low-grade sense that things are fragile — that the rules keep shifting, that the floor might drop, that you’re one mistake away from trouble. When you live with that kind of pressure, certainty feels like relief. Even a brittle certainty. Even a certainty that requires you to flatten other people into caricatures.

And then there’s the darker bargain: the stories that travel the fastest often offer you a little jolt of power.

Not real power — not the power to fix anything or make life wider — but the power of contempt. The power of being “above” someone. The power of turning a complicated person into a symbol and then enjoying how clean the world feels when you don’t have to be curious anymore.

Bad-faith negativity thrives on that. It doesn’t just say “this is wrong.” It says: *stay hot.* Stay suspicious. Stay entertained. Treat cruelty like honesty and call it “just telling it like it is.”

I’m trying not to pretend I’m immune to the temptation.

Sometimes I can feel how easy it would be to let cynicism do the driving. If I expect the worst, I don’t have to risk disappointment. If I stay sarcastic, I don’t have to admit I’m scared. If I keep other people small in my head, I don’t have to wrestle with how complicated it is to be human right now.

But cynicism isn’t clarity. It’s just a way of protecting yourself by shrinking your capacity to care.

One thing that helps me is to listen for the human sentence underneath the talking point.

When I see a story that feels like it’s recruiting me — when I can feel my fingers itching to share it, dunk it, or file it away as proof that “those people” are hopeless — I try to translate.

What might the person passing this around actually be trying to say?

Sometimes it’s something like: I don’t feel safe. I don’t feel respected. I don’t trust the people in charge. I’m tired. I’m lonely. I’m watching my life get more expensive and I don’t know who’s telling the truth about why.

Those sentences don’t automatically produce good ideas. They don’t excuse harm. But they’re closer to reality than the armor.

I think about this a lot building software in healthcare, because you can watch “narration” happen in miniature.

When a workflow breaks, the fastest explanation is personal: the user did it wrong, the team didn’t think, somebody failed. That story is emotionally efficient. It gives you a culprit and lets you move on.

But when you slow down, the more accurate story is usually structural: language that assumes too much, a handoff that doesn’t hold under stress, incentives that reward speed over care, an “edge case” that’s actually just someone’s real day.

If you want a fix that lasts, you have to keep the person in view. You have to stay curious long enough to see the constraint, not just the mistake.

I keep wishing for that kind of curiosity in public life. Not the performative “both sides” kind. The honest kind that can name harm without needing humiliation as proof. The kind that can admit uncertainty without collapsing into passivity.

So today my small practice is simple: when a story shows up with a ready-made villain, I try to ask what it’s doing to my heart before I let it move my hands.

Is it making me more accurate, or just more certain?

Is it helping me see people, or helping me enjoy not seeing them?

I don’t have a grand answer for where everything is headed. Mostly I’m trying to protect a slower attention — the kind that doesn’t get easily rented out by the loudest narrative in the room.

If nothing else, I want my clarity to cost less. Less contempt. Less reflexive certainty. Less turning people into props.

Reality is still made of people. I’m trying to live like that’s true.
